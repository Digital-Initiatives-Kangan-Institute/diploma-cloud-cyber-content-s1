### C1 — Preparing the walkthrough
- Teaches: [ICTCLD501 PC 5.1]
- Kicker: one point per part, and the hard questions first
- [PRIMER] What an approval walkthrough is
  - A walkthrough presents the design and plan to the people who must approve them, for a decision.
  - Speak to your choices and their rationale — the decisions that matter, not every line.
  - Approval is a conversation: they will probe, you will respond.
  image: none
  notes:
    Purpose is approval, not information — managers approve decisions, so present decisions.
    Question to pose: fifteen minutes, thirty pages — what do you actually talk about?
- [BESPOKE] Plan the order, and the one point per part
  - Decide the order you take the client through the two documents.
  - For each part, write the one point they must understand — if they remember one thing per section, which thing?
  - Time it against the slot you have; running out of time is the most common way this goes wrong.
  image: none
  notes:
    Workbook task 38 — the walkthrough plan. One point per part is the discipline that stops a page-turn.
    The timing warning comes from the workbook itself; make them rehearse against a clock.
- [BESPOKE] Anticipate the questions
  - Write the questions you would ask if you were the client, and your answer to each.
  - The ones worth preparing are where you made a choice someone could challenge.
  - If you justified the choice in the document, the answer is already written — find it.
  image: none
  notes:
    Workbook task 39. The challengeable choices are the ones with a rejected alternative — that's where questions land.
    Connects straight back to the justification tasks in Parts A and B.
- [EX] Plan and prepare
  - Workbook — tasks 38 and 39.
  - Plan the walkthrough order with the one point per part, then write the questions you'd ask in the client's place and your answers.
  timer: ~25 min
  image: none
  notes:
    Activity = practice workbook tasks 38–39.
    Press "one point per part" — most first drafts have five.
- [TAKEAWAYS] Section 1 · Preparation
  - Order, one point per part, timed to the slot.
  - Prepare the questions your own choices invite.
  image: none

### C2 — The walkthrough, and the feedback
- Teaches: [ICTCLD501 PC 5.1] · [ICTCLD501 PC 5.2]
- Kicker: present, listen, respond
- [BESPOKE] Conduct the walkthrough
  - Present the design and plan to the client, to your plan, inside the time.
  - Record the session immediately afterwards: when, who, and what was actually covered.
  - The record is evidence of the performance — written while you still remember it.
  image: none
  notes:
    Workbook task 40 — the live session plus its record. The record is filled in straight after, not reconstructed later.
    In class the client is played by the teacher or a classmate; both work.
- [BESPOKE] Seek feedback, and respond to it
  - Ask for feedback deliberately — don't wait for it to be offered.
  - Respond: accept and adjust, or defend with reasons. Both are professional; silence is neither.
  - Record what was said and what you did about it.
  image: none
  notes:
    Workbook task 41. Whoever plays the client gives at least one thing to change — that's the point of the exercise.
    Caving to every comment reads as no conviction; defending everything reads as not listening. Either extreme is the coaching moment.
- [EX] Run the walkthrough
  - Workbook — tasks 40 and 41.
  - Conduct the walkthrough in pairs, record the session, then record the feedback you sought and how you responded.
  timer: ~35 min
  image: none
  notes:
    Activity = practice workbook tasks 40–41, in pairs, both roles.
    The approver probes the choices and offers at least one change; the presenter answers from their own justifications.
- [TAKEAWAYS] Section 2 · The session
  - Present to plan, inside the time, and record it immediately.
  - Seek the feedback; accept or defend with reasons; record both.
  image: none

### C3 — Lodgement and sign-off
- Teaches: [ICTCLD501 PC 5.3] · [ICTCLD501 PC 5.4]
- Kicker: filed where policy says, signed by who policy names
- [BESPOKE] Lodge the plan
  - The organisation's records policy says where a completed engagement document goes — read it, and follow it.
  - Lodgement is what makes the plan findable in a disaster; a plan nobody can find recovers nothing.
  - Record where you lodged it and under what policy.
  image: none
  notes:
    Workbook task 42 — lodgement per the organisation's Records Management Policy, not to personal preference.
    The policy is on the intranet; the task is reading it and doing what it says.
- [BESPOKE] Obtain sign-off
  - Ask for the decision, and record it: approved, approved with conditions, or not yet.
  - Approval with conditions is a real outcome, not a failure — record the conditions and what happens next.
  - Sign-off closes the engagement; the record of it is the evidence.
  image: none
  notes:
    Workbook task 43. Conditions are the realistic outcome worth normalising — the workbook says so explicitly.
    The recorded decision, not the handshake, is what the assessor reads.
- [EX] Lodge and close
  - Workbook — tasks 42 and 43.
  - Work out the lodgement the records policy requires and do it, then ask for sign-off and record the decision.
  timer: ~20 min
  image: none
  notes:
    Activity = practice workbook tasks 42–43.
    Make them cite the policy by name in the lodgement record.
- [TAKEAWAYS] Topic 5 · Key takeaways
  - One point per part; prepare the questions your choices invite.
  - Present, seek feedback, respond with reasons, record everything.
  - Lodge to policy; record the decision, conditions and all.
  image: none
